# Money: Adversarial Review of the Design

**Date:** 2026-11-18
**Reviewed:** `2026-11-18-money-design.md` (and the plan, `2026-11-18-money.md`, written after these fixes)
**Reviewer:** the design's own author (Claude), working against it with a hostile brief. This is **not** an independent review. An independent pass (superpowers:code-reviewer) on the PR is still worth doing. Nothing has been built.

## Method

1. Tried to make each activity fail or mislead a child, a parent and the build.
2. Wrote scratch scripts (kept outside the repo) that load the draft item list (40 items, then a 27-item core) and ran the design's own rules against it: pair counts per level, how often the dearer item is also the bigger one, sort sets solvable by size alone, "Can you buy it?" candidates per budget, and Money Wall arithmetic.
3. Read the hub, `build.sh`, `ci.yml` and `shared/storage.js` for everything a sixth game touches.

**Proven by a run:** findings 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 20. **Proven by reading the code or docs:** 3, 12, 19. **Reasoned only:** the rest. The prices are my estimates and have **not** been looked up; that is the largest open risk (finding 14).

## Findings

Severity: **High** (wrong or disappointing result), **Medium** (rework or silent failure), **Low** (friction). "Fixed" means the design or plan now reflects it.

| # | Sev | Finding | Evidence | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High | **A child can win by always tapping the bigger thing.** Houses, cars and tractors are big and dear; sweets and ice cream are small and cheap. | Run: with the draft list the dearer item is also the physically bigger one in 61% of easy pairs (only 6% of pairs have a strictly smaller dearer item with the first list), and 72% of easy 3-item sort sets are already in price order when put in size order. The Maths audit already guards its own version of this | **Fixed.** Added small-but-dear items (phone, console, tablet) and big-but-cheap ones (paddling pool, tent, cardboard box, Christmas tree). The sampler has quotas: at least 40% of pairs and 50% of sort sets must not be solvable by size. Task 4 tests them in a 20,000-round audit. Feasible: 115 easy pairs qualify on the 40-item list, 82 on the 27-item core |
| 2 | High | **The Money Wall lies about cheap things next to expensive ones.** The wall scales to the dearer item (at most 3,000 squares). Beside a £50,000 tractor each square is £100, and "minimum one square" would draw a £1 bag of sweets as £100. | Run: sweets vs tractor and sweets vs house both give 0.01 of a square | **Fixed.** Anything under half a square is drawn as a tiny dot with the caption, never a square (`wallFor` in Task 6; tested). Bike (£150) next to a house is 1.5 squares and rounds to 2 |
| 3 | High | **The hub cannot hold a sixth tile as written.** The grid is a lead plus `--others: 4`, and the medium layout uses `calc(var(--others) / 2)` rows. | `hub.html` lines 28 to 33, 91 to 94, 109: five tiles total gives `--others: 5` and 2.5 rows | **Fixed in the plan.** Task 16 reworks the layout at four viewports, with a screenshot check. Not run |
| 4 | Medium | **Two budgets make "Can you buy it?" a trick.** £2 has one affordable item (sweets), so it is the answer every time. £10,000 and £20,000 have 36 of 40 items affordable and 2 to 3 clearly too dear, so the answer is always "nearly everything". | Run: per-budget counts of clearly affordable and clearly too-dear items | **Fixed.** Budgets are £5, £10 (easy), £20, £50, £100, £200 (medium), £200, £500, £1,000, £5,000 (hard). Task 5 re-runs the counts on the real item list and fails if any budget cannot make a round with at least 1 affordable and 2 too-dear items |
| 5 | Medium | **The apple (50p) cannot be shown on the wall** and was the only item under £1. | Reasoning | **Fixed.** Dropped; chocolate bar (£1.20, whole-pound rule excludes it from How many) added to batch 2 |
| 6 | Medium | **40 drawings, plus coins, notes, activity pictures and a hub mark, is the biggest cost and the biggest review burden.** | Run: a 27-item core still gives 188 easy / 265 medium / 298 hard pairs with 44 to 49% not-bigger, and every item appears in a not-bigger pair | **Fixed.** v1 ships on a 27-item core; 12 more are an optional second batch. The engine and validator do not care how many items there are |
| 7 | Medium | **"Used car" and "new car" look identical at 56px**, so a hard pair of them would be unanswerable from the picture. | Reasoning, then re-run | **Fixed.** One "car" (£15,000, range £8,000 to £30,000). The 27-item core was re-run after this change. A `group` field also stops near-duplicates (sweets, chocolate; bike, scooter) being paired |
| 8 | Medium | **A five-item sort reveal cannot hold five walls at 360px**, and "Can you buy it?" has nothing to wall (it is about a budget). | Reasoning (layout), not run | **Decision (author's call, Rob can overrule):** the sort reveal shows the correct order with price tags plus walls for the cheapest and dearest only; the budget reveal shows ticks, crosses and price tags with no wall. Task 11 and Task 12 test it at 360 by 640 |
| 9 | Medium | **Hidden prices can leak.** An `aria-label`, `title`, `alt` or `data-price` that names the price before the child answers gives the answer away (to a screen reader, and to a child who reads text). | Reasoning, from how Maths once printed its own answer | **Fixed in the plan.** Task 15's play-test asserts that, until the child answers, no element's text, `aria-label`, `title` or `data-*` attribute contains a price, a count or an order number, in the three guess activities |
| 10 | Medium | **The wall is decoration, so it needs a text alternative.** Colour, a square count and a dot size mean nothing to a screen reader. | Reasoning | **Fixed.** The wall is `aria-hidden`; the price tag beside it says "about £3,000, which is 3,000 pound coins" and is announced with `KidsGames.say` |
| 11 | Medium | **How many £1 coins? with £1.50, £2 or 50p items has no clean answer**, and a distractor 10 times below the answer could be under 1. | Reasoning | **Fixed.** Only whole-pound items of £3 or more; distractors come from 10x and 100x either side and are never below 1; the correct count is the smallest, middle or largest about a third of the time each. All tested |
| 12 | Medium | **A new game touches six files that list games by hand.** | `build.sh` lines 55 (preflight), 98 (tests), 453 to 541 (build), 631 (release zip), the `_cleanup` trap variables; `ci.yml` line 74 (hard-coded game list); README | **Fixed.** Task 1 makes every change and checks the build before any game code exists. Because it touches `build.sh` and `.github/`, the first PR runs the browser job for **all** games (about 3 minutes, in parallel) |
| 13 | Low | **Tap-in-order lets a child brute-force the sort.** | Reasoning | Accepted: Sentence Blocks sets the precedent that retrying in a learning game is fine. Only the **first** wrong tap in each position counts as a miss, so a child cannot make the "tricky" list worse by exploring (tested) |
| 14 | Medium | **Prices were my estimates.** UK prices drift, differ by shop and by family. A wrong band quietly teaches the wrong thing. | I did not check any price at first | **Mostly closed.** Web-checked 2026-11-18 (general, not exact). It changed 17 of 27 items, some a lot: house £300,000 to £275,000, car £15,000 to £17,000, games console £400 to £500 (PS5 is £570 after the April 2026 rise), scooter £50 to £70, tent £60 to £120, bike £150 to £180, sofa £700 to £800, phone range widened. **Still estimates:** sweets, ice lolly, ice cream, comic, balloon, beach ball, cardboard box, football, teddy (not found online). Ranges stay wide, bands must not overlap, every reveal says "about", each item has an `asOf` date. Rob reviews the final table (Task 2) |
| 15 | Low | **A miss in "which costs more" has two possible culprits.** The child may have overestimated the cheap item or underestimated the dear one. | Reasoning | Both items get the miss. It is only a hint for what comes back, not a score |
| 16 | Medium | **Is this better than telling the twins "a house is 300,000 sweets" at the table?** The game gives repeated, steered practice, but it cannot anchor to what *their* family actually spends. | Adversary's question | **Partly answered.** The item list is a plain data file with a validator, so Rob can add the things the twins ask for. Open question for Rob (design section 8, question 3): do the twins have pocket money, so budgets can match it |
| 17 | Low | **Drawn "pound coins" must not look like real currency.** | Reasoning (the Bank of England restricts reproducing notes) | **Avoided for v1.** The budget is a wallet drawing and a number, so no coin or note is drawn. When coins and change arrive (v1.1) they will be plain value tokens with no portrait or security features |
| 18 | Low | **Wording can shame.** "Can't afford it", "poor", "rich", "cheap" as an insult. | P4 | **Fixed.** Copy rule in the plan: "Not enough", "costs more", "about". A test scans all UI strings for a banned-word list |
| 19 | High | **Yellow is not free.** The design gave Money the yellow paint as "the only unused one". | `DESIGN.md` line 129: Playground Yellow is the system's own paint (default primary action, selected-player highlight), owned by no game; `tokens.css` uses it for `--accent-tertiary` and text selection | **Fixed.** Money gets a new sixth game paint (a magenta-pink is recommended), added to `tokens.css` in both the Day and Dusk sets and to `DESIGN.md`, with contrast checked by script. A shared-file change, so every game's browser job runs. **Decided: Rob chose the magenta-pink (2026-11-18)** |
| 20 | Medium | **New activity, What's the price?: three ways to go wrong.** (a) A wide-range item (a sofa, £300 to £2,000) on a 1.5x ladder has most of the six tiles "right". (b) The right tile could be the only "round" number. (c) Points could turn a learning game into a contest. | Run (scratch copy): with fixed steps a tractor at medium had 5 full-point tiles; with the fix, none of 2,000 seeds per item per level breaks the rules | **Fixed.** Ladder step is at least the square root of `hi / lo`; every tile (including the right one) is rounded to 2 significant figures; points only compare a child with their own best. The audit (7 tests) and a mutation check cover (a) and (b); (c) is Rob's call (he asked for points) |
